<Summary>
- **As of 28 October 2026, QuickNode is the fastest private RPC we measure:** a 7.2 ms median round trip, the lowest median in 18 of 27 chain and region cells (one shared with Chainstack) and the lowest p99 in 21. Over WebSocket it pushes new blocks first in 13 of 18 cells.
- **BlockMachine is the cheapest and the slowest.** A dapp workload costs $25 a month for 10M requests and $999 for 1B, against $130 and $9,999 at QuickNode. Its median round trip from US East is 113 ms.
- **Chainstack is the value pick.** $49 covers 20M dapp requests, and from US East it is the fastest provider of all (2.9 ms median). From Singapore its median rises to 11.5 ms.
- <Mark>Location changes the ranking more than the provider does.</Mark> Dwellir answers in 3.8 ms from Singapore and 75 ms from US East (medians); Chainstack is four times slower from Singapore than from Virginia.
- The same 100M dapp requests cost between $125 and $1,255 a month across the six. Trace is the exception to BlockMachine's lead: Dwellir is cheapest there, $299 at 100M.
</Summary>

## Speed against price

No provider is both fast and cheap. The two cheapest, BlockMachine and Dwellir, are the two slowest; the fastest, QuickNode, bills $1,049 for 100M dapp requests. Chainstack and GetBlock sit between, with Chainstack the better trade on both axes.

What the medians hide, provider by provider:

- **[QuickNode](/products/quicknode)** serves Arc and Robinhood from outside Asia, so those two chains read 163 ms and 66 ms from Singapore. Its Ethereum p99 from Singapore is 176 ms.
- **[Chainstack](/products/chainstack)** is the fastest provider from US East, 2.4 to 3.1 ms on six chains. From Singapore it reads 39 ms on Ethereum and Robinhood, 70 ms on HyperEVM and 83 ms on Polygon.
- **[GetBlock](/products/getblock)** never has the fastest median but is never far off, except on HyperEVM from US East (235 ms). It returned 88 stale blocks in 7 days, the most in the cohort.
- **[Alchemy](/products/alchemy)**'s 55% success rate is our plan, not its network. The Free key on seven chains hit its 30M CU cap on 24 October and has failed every call since. On HyperEVM and Robinhood, where we hold an enterprise key, it answered 100% of calls.
- **[Dwellir](/products/dwellir)** answers in about 75 ms from US East on five of its eight chains and 96 ms on Arbitrum; Base and Robinhood are the exceptions at 2.4 to 2.7 ms. A new connection costs it a median of 113 to 333 ms depending on region.
- **[BlockMachine](/products/blockmachine)** is the slowest in every region, best from EU West (42 ms median), and had the most timeouts: 153 in four days. It joined the cohort on 24 October.

<LatencyHeatmap />

Sub-10 ms values mostly measure the distance between our probe and the provider's nearest node. From another city the absolute figures change; the order within a region is what carries over.

## WebSocket push

QuickNode delivers new blocks first in 13 of 18 chain and region cells, on Base, BNB Chain, Polygon and Arc by a wide margin. The exceptions are Ethereum, where GetBlock's regional sockets win from US East (97%) and Singapore (76%), and HyperEVM, where each region has a different leader.

<WsFirstChart />

- **Chainstack's Polygon socket trails the first push by 0.75 to 1 s** at the median in every region. It also dropped the most connections: 23 in 7 days, against 3 for QuickNode and 2 for GetBlock.
- **Dwellir's Ethereum pushes arrive 0.47 to 0.71 s late** at the median and up to 1.6 s at p90.
- **HyperEVM has no reliable leader.** Even the winning provider's p90 lag runs 170 to 350 ms in each region.

Alchemy left the WebSocket race on 15 October, because it bills each pushed message and the Free key could not carry it; BlockMachine has not been added yet. Solana, Arbitrum and Robinhood were taken out of the race on 17 October: they produced 73% of all pushes, and one provider's monthly message limit had been reached.

## Cost by budget

Between the cheapest and the dearest of the six, the monthly bill differs by 5x at 1M requests and by 13x at 1B. Price and speed run in opposite directions: QuickNode is the second dearest from 100M up.

| <L s="alchemy">[Alchemy](/products/alchemy)</L> | $13 | $125 | $627 | $1,255 | $12,548 |

Across the full 19-provider pricing cohort, BlockMachine is the cheapest dapp, indexer and archive option at 10M, 100M and 1B requests. BlockPI undercuts it in between (dapp at 50M, $108) and on simple reads at 10M ($24). On trace, Dwellir leads from 100M and ties Chainstack and dRPC at $49 for 10M. On Solana, Syndica is cheapest from 100M ($199).

Four mechanics explain most of these numbers:

- **Unit weights, not headline prices.** Dwellir and BlockMachine bill one unit per response, archive included. Alchemy charges 26 CU for an `eth_call`, so a dapp request averages 23.9 CU. QuickNode charges 20 credits per Ethereum call and 30 on Solana.
- **Trace reverses BlockMachine's lead.** It meters a trace call at 25 Request Units: $429 for 10M trace requests, against $49 at Dwellir or Chainstack.
- **Chainstack moves in steps.** $49 includes 20M Request Units, exactly 10M archive requests at 2 RU each. At 10.1M the bill is $52, at 11M $79, at 15M $199.
- **Throughput caps come before price limits.** Alchemy pay-as-you-go stops at 30,000 CU a second and BlockMachine at 4,167 RU a second, so neither can serve 500M trace requests a month at any price.

These are list prices, not invoices. Alchemy and QuickNode return no usage header, so we cannot reconcile their billed units with our own traffic.

## Free plans

Free plans cover a prototype, not a product. Among the six, only Chainstack's free plan carries more than 1.5M dapp requests a month; dRPC, outside our latency cohort, carries the most at 10.5M.

<FreeTierChart />

QuickNode offers a one-month trial that does not renew, so it has no ranked free plan. Dwellir's free plan gives 100,000 responses a day but excludes `eth_getLogs`, which the dapp mix calls.

The allowance is rarely the binding limit. GetBlock's 50,000 CU a day lasts 125 seconds at its own advertised 20 requests a second; Infura's daily credits last 1.7 hours at full rate. Our own Alchemy Free key ran out on 24 October and has refused every call on seven chains since.

## Questions

### Which private RPC provider is the fastest?

QuickNode. Over the seven days to 28 October 2026 it had the lowest HTTP median in 18 of 27 chain and region cells (one shared with Chainstack) and the lowest p99 in 21, with a median round trip of 7.2 ms across nine chains and three regions. It also pushed new blocks first over WebSocket in 13 of 18 cells.

### Which private RPC provider is the cheapest?

On a dapp workload, BlockMachine: $25 a month for 10M requests, $125 for 100M and $999 for 1B, against $130, $1,049 and $9,999 at QuickNode. For trace and debug calls Dwellir is cheaper, at $299 for 100M requests.

### What is the best RPC provider for under $50 a month?

Chainstack. Its $49 Growth plan covers 20M dapp requests a month, or 10M archive and trace requests, and from US East it had the lowest median latency of all six providers, 2.9 ms.

### Is a free RPC plan enough for production?

Rarely. The most generous free plan we price, dRPC, buys 10.5M dapp requests a month and Chainstack's buys 3.0M. Our own Alchemy Free key reached its 30M CU monthly cap on 24 October 2026 and refused every call on seven chains after that.

## Method

| Measurement | How | Window |
|---|---|---|
| HTTP latency | `eth_getBlockByNumber` (`getSlot` on Solana) every 120 s per provider, chain and region. Calls alternate between a pooled connection (warm) and a new DNS, TCP and TLS connection (cold). Each answer is classed ok, http_err, jsonrpc_err, stale or timeout. | 21 Oct 2026 13:00 to 28 Oct 2026 13:00 UTC |
| WebSocket push | One `eth_subscribe(newHeads)` socket per provider and region, ten minutes in every hour. Each block is scored against the first provider to push it in that region. GetBlock issues one socket per chain and region and has none for HyperEVM in US East and Singapore or for Arc in Singapore. | Same |
| Cost | Each provider's published unit weights applied to fixed method mixes, cheapest plan that can serve the volume, throughput caps enforced. | Catalogue of 22 Oct 2026 |
| Free plans | Each renewing free allowance converted into requests of the same mixes. | Same |

Probes run from US East (Virginia), EU West (Amsterdam) and Singapore. Latency is the 7-day percentile of raw probe samples, steadier than the 24-hour figure on the bench pages. Validation Cloud left the cohort on 18 October after its key returned 401 on every call.

The probes send one cheap call; a heavy method or another location can change the order. Prices exclude negotiated discounts, and dRPC prices are an upper bound because it routes to operators who may charge less.
